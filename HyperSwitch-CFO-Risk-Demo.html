Win 1: Recover Revenue We Are Losing

How HyperSwitch captures it

The Revenue Recovery module is an ML-driven retry engine with 30+ decision parameters. It addresses exactly the gaps the retry-modifications proposal flagged:

  • Delays same-order retries by 48-72h instead of failing immediately
  • Auto-escalates failed same-order retries to new-order retries (77% recovery vs 11%)
  • Filters dead subscriptions out of the retry pool (45% of current retries are dead)
  • Code-aware retry decisions, not just time-based
The retry analysis already done by Payment Ops shows the lift is real. HyperSwitch is the engine to capture it.
Win 2: Own The Customer Card Vault
Strategic Leverage
Vault
Portability
Switch processors without re-enrolling customers

Why this matters operationally

Today, our card-on-file tokens live at each PSP. NMI holds US tokens. WorldPay holds EU tokens. If we want to change processors in either market, we have to either re-enroll every customer or accept a major drop in subscription continuity.

HyperSwitch stores the actual card data (PAN + Network Transaction ID + network tokens) in a PCI Level 1 vault that Unicity controls. Once a customer's card is in our vault, we can route their next charge to ANY processor without the customer noticing.

That is real leverage at the negotiating table.

Apache 2.0 open source. Self-hostable in Unicity infrastructure. Vendor lock-in eliminated.

Likely CFO + Risk questions and prepared answers. Skim before the meeting.

Q: What is the total commitment?
A: 2 years on the Enterprise license at $7,000/month, paid monthly. After 2 years we have two choices: renew Enterprise at a re-negotiated rate, or move to Community Edition at $0 license cost. There is no 3-year lock-in.
Q: What if Juspay goes out of business?
A: HyperSwitch is Apache 2.0 open source. We have the source code. Worst case we maintain our own fork. The product itself does not disappear if the vendor does.
Q: What is the actual cost per transaction?
A: At our current volume (about 1.4-1.5M orders/year), roughly $0.13 per transaction in Year 1 for the Enterprise package with all recommended add-ons. By Year 3 onwards it drops to about $0.06 per transaction once the license fee ends. For comparison, payment processors themselves typically charge $0.10-$0.30 per transaction before interchange.
Q: How do we know the Revenue Recovery will work?
A: The opportunity is already quantified. The Section 5.39 / TKT-6827 analysis shows the gap exists in our current data ($1.0M-$1.7M / year). The question isn't whether the opportunity is real - it's whether HyperSwitch's Revenue Recovery captures it. We'll measure with a 6-month pilot before committing to broad deployment.
Q: What does PCI compliance look like?
A: The HyperSwitch vault is PCI DSS Level 1. If we self-host it in Unicity infrastructure, we add a PCI audit cost of about $5K/year. If a customer's card touches our vault, our PCI scope expands but the vault product itself is built for that scope. No different from how Stripe or any other tokenization service handles it.
Q: How long until we see results?
A: 6-8 weeks to deploy the sandbox + first connector. 3-4 months to migrate first market to production. 6 months to see measurable approval-rate uplift. The Section 5.39 retry-program improvements would phase in as Revenue Recovery comes online.
Q: What happens to our existing processors?
A: They stay. HyperSwitch is a layer ABOVE the processors, not a replacement. We keep WorldPay, NMI, Nuvei, Trust, Braintree, 2C2P. HyperSwitch decides which one each transaction goes to. Adding or removing processors becomes a configuration change instead of a code project.
Q: Why not just hire engineers to build this in-house?
A: We could, but it would take 18-24 months and roughly $500K-$1M in engineering cost to reach feature parity. HyperSwitch already exists, already processes $1T/year at Juspay, and the Apache 2.0 license means we never pay for the underlying technology. We pay for the speed-to-market and the support.
Q: What's the worst-case scenario for risk?
A: We spend roughly $400K over 2 years (license + setup + ops + add-ons) and Revenue Recovery doesn't deliver. In that case, we still gained: vault portability, faster market expansion, failover protection, and a modern routing engine. We then drop to Community Edition at $0 license and keep the product. We can't lose more than the 2 years of license fees.
