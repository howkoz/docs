The payments portal: what is working, and what Payments runs on it

The portal is where Payments sets up and runs the in-house payment service without a developer: connect processor accounts, decide where each payment goes, preview the payment form, watch every payment and webhook, and manage keys and people. Dashboard menu › Payments, Orders You see and search

Payments and Orders

Payments lists every payment opened in the chosen mode over the range, figures first, then the payments. Orders groups the payments under the order reference our systems gave them, one row per order. Open any row for everything about it.

What you can do here

  • Filter by market, processor and status ("Every market", "Every processor")
  • Search Orders by order reference, payment id, a card's last four digits or the shopper's email
  • Sort and page through the list
  • Open a payment: details, shopper, metadata, card replacements
  • See every attempt on it, which processor account took it, and the outcome
  • Each decline is labelled hard ("the same card will not succeed later") or soft ("the same card could succeed later")
  • See when a payment set up a stored card for later rebills, and any 3-D Secure challenge

What one payment shows

Shopperemail, billing address
Attemptseach try, account used, outcome
Declinehard or soft, the processor's reason
Replacementscard replaced on the same payment
Metadatatags our checkout sent

Demo it

  1. Open Payments, filter Market = Canada.
  2. Open one payment and scroll to Attempts."Every try, the account it went to, and why it ended."
  3. Open Orders and search an order number."Customer service can find an order from an email or last four digits."

Payment processors

Payments connects our processor accounts here, with their credentials, and decides what each account takes: which markets, which currency in each market, which card brands and which wallets. No developer is needed to add another merchant account.

What you can do here

  • Connect a processor: Worldpay, NMI, Nuvei, PayPal or Braintree, in Test or Live
  • Give it a label and an account type: online, phone and mail, or rebills
  • Enter its credentials; secrets are never shown again, leave blank to keep them
  • Choose its markets and the currency it charges in each
  • Choose its ways to pay: card brands, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, with each wallet's card networks
  • Worldpay accounts: turn on extended decline codes and set 3-D Secure
  • Test connection at any time: "Credentials work" or "Credentials refused"
  • Enable or disable an account with one switch
  • See which routing rules use the account before changing it
  • Delete an account that has never taken a payment; disable one that has

Checkout New 10 Nov You see and try

Payment form

The hosted payment form exactly as a shopper sees it in each market: the ways to pay that market offers, the card fields, and the billing address in that country's own fields. You can type into it and check it; nothing is sent.

What you can do here

  • Pick any of the 50 markets from a searchable list with flags
  • Read, in one sentence, how shoppers there can pay: in Canada, by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal
  • See the methods shown in the order set for the form
  • Switch the preview between Desktop and Mobile size
  • Change the billing country and see its address fields
  • Turn on "Highlight my changes" to see each field our settings changed, and why
  • Follow the link to Processors to add a way to pay a market lacks

Built alongside it on 10 Nov: payment method settings per market (global, then per market) and the order the form offers them in. Fidel Bello's thread of 10 Nov: the portal screen for changing that order is the next piece being built.

Demo it

  3. Pick United Kingdom."The UK form offers cards only, because that is what the UK account takes."

  5. Turn on Highlight my changes.
Routing You set up and change

Routing rules

Which of our processor accounts charges each payment. Pick a market and a kind of payment; the rules are checked top to bottom, the first that matches decides, and the fallback takes every payment no rule matches. Rules are drafted, tried, then published, and every version is kept.

Published today (Test mode)

MarketOnline payments
USVersion 2, 3 Nov: cards to NMI 100%
CanadaVersion 1, 2 Nov: cards to Nuvei; Apple Pay and Google Pay to Worldpay Canada; PayPal to Braintree; fallback Nuvei. Draft saved 5 Nov cards 80% Nuvei / 20% Worldpay, failover on. Rebills: version 1, 2 Nov, cards to Nuvei
UKVersion 2, 2 Nov: four brands to Worldpay GBP 100%, fallback Worldpay GBP

43 dry runs on 10 Nov across these versions and the Canada draft: every payment went exactly where the rules said.

What you can do here

  • Pick the market and the kind of payment: online, phone and mail, or rebills
  • Add, name, reorder (up and down) and remove rules
  • Conditions on a rule: payment method (card or a wallet), currency, amount (at least, more than, less than, at most), card brand, customer reference, or a tag our checkout sends
  • Send a rule to one account or split it across several by share %
  • Turn failover on per rule: if the processor itself fails, the payment moves to the next account
  • Pick a fallback account for anything no rule matches, or refuse it
  • Set the attempts allowed per payment
  • Copy rules from another kind of payment
  • "Where payments go": the portal lists, for every currency, brand and wallet, which rule or fallback takes it, and warns of anything the rules would refuse
  • Try a payment: enter amount, currency, brand or wallet and see which account takes it, under the published version or the draft. Nothing is charged
  • Save draft, discard draft, publish; history shows who drafted and published each version

Settings menu › Settings You set up and change

Settings

Our company's name, the websites the payment form may be shown on, the keys our servers hold, and a link to webhooks. Changes take effect at once: the next form and the next message use them.

What you can do here

  • Change the company name
  • Set the allowed origins: the websites allowed to show the payment form (today the developers' demo shop and unicity-checkout.vercel.app)
  • API keys: generate one for Test and one for Live. A key is shown once, when made; rotating gives the old key 24 hours before it stops
  • See when each key was made and last used
  • Change your own password (at least 12 characters); your other sessions are signed out

Settings menu › Team New 10 Nov You set up and change

Team

Everyone who signs in for our company, and their role. An admin invites teammates by link, as a member or an admin, and can make a member an admin. Each person gets their own login, so every change carries a name.

What you can do here

  • See everyone who can sign in, their role and when they joined
  • Invite a teammate: choose member or admin, then send the link. It works once, for seven days
  • Promote a member to admin
  • Any button a person's role does not allow is greyed out and says which permission it needs

The two roles

Member"Sees everything and drafts routing rules; changes nothing else."
Admin"Also changes your setup: processors, routing, webhooks, keys, settings and the team."

Demo it

  1. Open Settings menu › Team.
  2. Click Invite a teammate, show the member and admin choice, then close."A member can draft routing for an admin to publish."

For the update meeting

Demo script, about 12 minutes

Run it in Test mode, signed in as an admin. Every step is read-only or a dry run; nothing below charges a card or changes a setting.

  1. Header (1 minute). Point at the tabs and the Test / Live switch."Everything Payments needs to run the service is in these tabs."
  2. Processors (2 minutes). The six accounts; open Nuvei Canada; open Connect a processor and back out."We connect and switch accounts ourselves."
  3. Checkout (2 minutes). Canada, then UK; switch to Mobile."What the shopper sees, market by market."
  4. Routing (3 minutes). Canada online: the published version, the draft, "Where payments go", two Try a payment runs, the version history."We decide where every payment goes, test it first, and keep every version."
  5. Dashboard, Payments, Orders (2 minutes). 30 days; open one payment's attempts; search an order.
  6. Webhooks (1 minute). Endpoints verified; open one delivery.
  7. Settings and Team (1 minute). Allowed websites, keys shown once, the member and admin roles.

Three lines to close on

  • Payments connects and manages processor accounts in the portal, with no developer
  • Payments sets, tests and publishes routing per market, with every version kept
  • Every payment, attempt and webhook is visible to Payments in one place
Keep the demo read-only: do not press Publish, Rotate (keys or secrets), Delete, or Save on Settings during the meeting. Try a payment, Test connection, opening drawers and switching tabs are all safe.

Acronyms on this page

API: application programming interface (how our website talks to the payment service). NMI: Network Merchants Inc., the gateway with Nuvei behind it for US cards. CAD, GBP, USD: Canadian dollar, British pound, US dollar. 3-D Secure: the card bank's check of the shopper. Amex: American Express. JCB: Japan Credit Bureau card brand. UK: United Kingdom. US: United States.
