Values were tested in memory and unique cards counted by a one-way fingerprint; nothing was kept or printed. The hard part: a clean 3,000-row sample is not a clean table. One sample missed 1.7 million cards, so every flagged column is counted in full.

Why it matters

Stored card numbers must be unreadable

Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) v4.0.1, Requirements 3.5.1 and 3.2.1
  • 3.5.1: wherever the full card number is stored, it is hashed, truncated, tokenised or strongly encrypted.
  • 3.2.1: a retention and disposal policy limits stored data to what the business needs.
  • 3.2.1: secure deletion when the data is no longer needed; age is no exception.
  • 3.2.1: a check at least every three months that data past retention is gone.
Paraphrased from the standard; check the exact wording against the published text before quoting it outward.

Where we stand

A readable card number is a gap whether it was written in 2004 or in 2025.
  • 1,128,817 readable card numbers, reachable with a read-only reporting login.

  • Backups hold more than any table and were not read.
